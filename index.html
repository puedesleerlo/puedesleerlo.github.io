I The question Physics, philosophy, risk, governance. Four notations, one problem.

Four notations, one problem

Every role on this page is the same question written in a different notation. Physics taught me that the mathematics does not care about the substrate — a Markov process is indifferent to whether its state is a particle or a borrower. Philosophy taught me the harder half: what counts as evidence at all, and how to decide about a technology when no evidence exists and the human cost is high.

Credit risk was the laboratory — uncertainty made explicit and priced. Then the pandemic moved micro and macro behavior in ways that did not correlate, and the models were still judged the way they always were: context-free, on their metrics. Technical metrics are not conclusive once the evidence base has gone. I answered with what I had — simulation, backtesting coverage pushed 20%→90%, thousands of runs a week — and still had to report an expected risk that could not be honestly derived from the evidence.

So I moved into public-sector governance to build the layer that was missing — a feedback and control loop bound to outcomes and context, not just to metrics — and built it for a city of 2.5M. On its own terms it worked. I still did not get the answer, which is the honest reason I am at Carnegie Mellon: I ran out of formal apparatus. What I want to understand is how to build environments that produce good decisions precisely where the human cost is high and the evidence is thin.
