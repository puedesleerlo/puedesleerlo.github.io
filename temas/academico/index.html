§1

About

I'm the data architect for the Subsecretaría de Espacio Público in Medellín, turning fifteen years of spreadsheets into a governed warehouse without stopping the operation for a day. Before that: river logbooks, plant-breeding niche models, edge data-acquisition boards, and a robotic arm. The subject keeps changing. The job doesn't — find the living system, build the instrument, leave a record someone else can check.

I care most about the boring half of that sentence: the record someone else can check. Most of what I build is provenance.

§6

Errata

Things I believed and then stopped believing. A CV lists what went right; this is the other column, and it's usually where the actual learning is.

  1. Thought the hard part of a data platform was the modelling. It's identity. Deciding that two rows describe the same person is most of the work.
  2. Cleaned data in views over the raw tables, because it was reversible. Reversible also means silently broken the day someone adds a column. Anonymisation belongs in the transformation.
  3. Assumed an LLM with database access needed a good system prompt to stay safe. It needs a database role. Behaviour is measured; permissions are guaranteed.
  4. Counted a geocoder's coverage as its quality. Half those coordinates arrived from a GPS device. Coverage without provenance is a number about the source, not the method.
  5. Built the pipeline first and the question bank afterwards. Backwards. The questions are the acceptance criteria — a mart should exist because something needs answering.
